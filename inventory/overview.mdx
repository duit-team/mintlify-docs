Inventory keeps **one balance per item per location**, and moves it only through documents: receive, sell, transfer, waste, count. Every document posts at its real cost (first in, first out), so your stock value and your cost of sales always agree.

## What kind of item is it?

| Kind | What it is | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| **Goods** | Bought and sold as-is | A bottle of water |
| **Ingredient** | Bought and used in recipes | Coffee beans, milk |
| **Prep** | Made in-house and kept in stock | A batch of sauce |
| **Dish** | Sold but never stocked | A latte |

A dish has no stock balance of its own. Selling one uses up its [recipe](/inventory/recipes) ingredients at the outlet.

## How stock moves

<Steps>
  <Step title="Receive">Goods arrive from a supplier into a warehouse or kitchen.</Step>
  <Step title="Transfer">Send stock to an outlet. It sits **in transit** until the outlet confirms what arrived. See [Transfers](/inventory/transfers).</Step>
  <Step title="Produce">A kitchen turns raw items into a prep item. See [Production](/inventory/production).</Step>
  <Step title="Sell, waste, count">Sales use up recipes, [waste](/inventory/waste) is written off with a reason, and a [stock count](/retail/stock-counts) corrects the rest.</Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  The POS never blocks a sale because of stock. If an item sells before its delivery is entered, the sale goes through, the item shows as negative on the [exceptions list](/inventory/usage-and-exceptions), and its cost is corrected when the delivery is entered.
</Note>
